Biodiversity in daily life

Biodiversity is the variety of living things in the natural world. It includes animals, plants, insects, fungi and microorganisms, as well as the ecosystems that connect them.

Protecting biodiversity is important because it helps ecosystems stay balanced and supports healthier places for both wildlife and people.

What biodiversity means

Biodiversity is not only about rare animals in distant places. It also includes trees, birds, insects, flowers and other living things found in local parks, gardens and green spaces.

  • It includes many different species
  • It includes the habitats they live in
  • It helps natural systems work properly

Why biodiversity matters

Healthy biodiversity supports pollination, cleaner air, stronger soils and more stable ecosystems. When ecosystems lose too many species, they become weaker and less able to recover from damage.

This matters to students too because greener and healthier spaces improve local environments and can make communities more attractive, pleasant and sustainable.

Main threats to life on land

Human activity can harm land through deforestation, pollution, littering, overbuilding and habitat destruction. Even small local problems can contribute to much bigger environmental damage over time.

  • Loss of habitats reduces shelter and food for wildlife
  • Pollution affects soil, water and plant growth
  • Damage to one species can affect the whole food chain

What students can do

Students can support life on land through realistic everyday actions. These do not need to be dramatic. Small repeated actions can still make a visible difference over time.

  • Reduce waste and recycle carefully
  • Respect parks and shared green spaces
  • Join planting projects
  • Polluting less by using public transport

Local impact and community value

Protecting local biodiversity supports birds, insects and plants while also making neighbourhoods cleaner and healthier. Life on land is therefore not just a global issue. It is also relevant to campuses, streets, school grounds and public spaces.

When local communities care for green areas, they help create better conditions for wildlife and a better environment for people at the same time.
